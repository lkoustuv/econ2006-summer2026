Scarcity & Choice

Every Choice Has a Cost

Scarcity means you always give something up when you make a choice. Every decision involves a trade-off between costs and benefits.

🎓 Example — Night before an exam

Study vs. sleep vs. going out — you can't do all three. Each choice has a cost.

🌽 Example — A farmer's decision

Plant corn or soybeans? The same land can't grow both at once.

Opportunity Cost

A formal way to measure the true cost of any choice — the best thing you give up.

8
Opportunity Cost

Definition

The value of the best alternative forgone when a choice is made.

💰 Example — $10,000 Decision

You have $10,000. Options: pay tuition · buy a car · invest in stocks · take a vacation.
If you choose tuition, the opportunity cost is the value of your single best remaining option — whichever of the others you valued most.

⚠ Opportunity cost is not the sum of all forgone options — only the best one.

Production Possibility Frontier

Setting Up the Model

A simple economy — 100 acres of land, two goods:

  • 🔫 Guns — 1 acre each → max 100
  • 🌹 Roses — ½ acre each → max 200
📊 PPF — Definition

All combinations of goods producible when all resources are used efficiently.

In this example, constant opportunity cost → straight line

Constant opportunity cost — straight line PPF

11
Production Possibility Frontier

Is the PPF Always a Straight Line?

In practice: No. Resources are not perfectly interchangeable — shifting more land to roses makes each additional rose progressively costlier.

  • Law of Increasing Opportunity Costs — each extra unit costs more of the other good
  • PPF bows outward from the origin
  • Slope of PPF = Marginal Rate of Transformation (MRT)

Same Δ roses → increasing Δ guns as you move down the PPF

12
Production Possibility Frontier

Where Can We Operate?

  • On the PPF — all resources used efficiently ✓
  • Inside the PPF — inefficient: resources are idle or technology underused

Why might we be inside?

  • Resources not fully employed (e.g., unemployment)
  • Technology not used to its full potential
🚀 Shifting Outward = Economic Growth

More resources or better technology expands the PPF — the economy can produce more of everything.

Points on, inside, and beyond the PPF

Scarcity & Choice

How Are Resources Allocated?

Given scarcity, which mechanism decides what gets produced and who gets it?

🏛️

Command Economy

Government decides what, how, and for whom to produce. Central planning replaces the market.

Examples: Former USSR · Cuba · North Korea

📈

Market Economy

Price is the key signal — it directs resources to their highest-valued uses through supply & demand.

Examples: Farmer's crop choice · Your choice of college major
